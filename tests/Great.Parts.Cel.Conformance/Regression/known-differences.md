# Known differences from cel-go

Divergences of Great.Parts.Cel from the pinned cel-go version that are analysed and are not counted as bugs of Great.Parts.Cel. Every entry has a signature, a class (`cel-go-bug` or `by-design`) and a reference: a link to the language definition or a minimal case. An entry of the class `cel-go-bug` that has been reported to cel-go also links its upstream issue.

| *(unknown, error) we-known | cel-go-bug | x + 1 / 0 with the attribute x unknown (the pattern x) is the error division by zero in the library and the unknown x in cel-go, and so is every call of two arguments whose first is unknown and whose second is an error (contains, math.bitOr, an index of a list whose elements are unknown, and the others): the language definition gives an error operand precedence over an unknown one in every case but a short-circuit of the logical operators (proto/cel/expr/eval.proto of cel.dev/expr v0.25.1, ExprValue: "Errors take precedence in all other cases: <unknown> + <error> -> <error>"), and cel-go returns the unknown first operand of a binary function after it has evaluated the second and dropped its error (interpreter/interpretable.go, evalBinary), and the unknown operand of an index that is not an attribute without evaluating the key (interpreter/attributes.go, relativeAttribute.Resolve). It is one cause for every function, so one row stands for all of them, with * in place of the function: the signature <call>(unknown, error) we-known is known for every call but the two logical operators, where cel-go and the library agree. A call of three arguments or more is no case of it: cel-go gives the error of any of them first (evalVarArgs). |
