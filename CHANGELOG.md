# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).
Until 1.0.0 the public API may change in any version.

A change of the result or of the error of an expression that was valid in an earlier version is listed under `Behavior changes` of its version.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The source generator reads a type context, a `partial` class derived from `CelTypeContext` with `[CelObject]` attributes, and reports a diagnostic for what it cannot describe: `GPCEL001` for a listed type of a kind that CEL cannot read, `GPCEL002` and `GPCEL007` (warnings) for a member that is left out, `GPCEL003`, `GPCEL004` and `GPCEL005` for two members or two types that get one name and for a name that is not valid, `GPCEL006` for a class that cannot be a context, `GPCEL008` for a C# language version below 12 and `GPCEL009` for a closure of more than 250 types. `CelObjectAttribute`, `CelTypeContextOptionsAttribute`, `CelIgnoreAttribute` and `CelNameAttribute` keep their arguments; a field name comes from `[CelName]`, then `[JsonPropertyName]` unless `IgnoreJsonAttributes` is set, then the naming policy `CamelCase`, `AsIs` or `SnakeCaseLower`, which give the names of the policies of System.Text.Json, and `[CelIgnore]` and `[JsonIgnore]` hide a member. A closed generic type has the default name `Acme.Page_Acme_Order`. See [Source generator diagnostics](docs/generator-diagnostics.md).
- The source generator writes the code of a type context: the `partial` class gets a private constructor and a static `Default` that holds its only instance, with the descriptions of the listed types and of the types their members reach — a class, a `record class`, a structure, an interface, a closed generic type, a nested type and a type of another assembly — and of the enumerations. A field is read by a direct access, without reflection and without boxing a number, so the descriptions work under Native AOT: the numbers, `string`, `Guid`, enumerations, bytes, time, `Nullable<>`, `object`, `JsonElement` and structures by the rows of the type mapping; arrays, lists and dictionaries as lazy views, sets as sorted copies and other collections as copies; a `Dictionary` in the order of its source. An `IList<T>` or an `IDictionary<K,V>` is wrapped when it is read only if the instance is not read-only already. A member that is `null` against its annotation is `UnexpectedNull` (3017), and a member that the generator left out is `FieldNotAccessible` (2007) with its reason. See [Types of a type context](docs/type-mapping.md#types-of-a-type-context), [Names of fields](docs/type-mapping.md#names-of-fields) and [Presence and null](docs/type-mapping.md#presence-and-null).
- The read of a member of a type that the source generator describes is charged beyond the unit of the selection when the evaluation has a cost limit: a member that is a structure that the read boxes costs 1 + ⌈S/64⌉ for a structure of S bytes, a set 1 + c · n · ⌈log₂(n + 1)⌉ for its sorted copy, and a collection without an index such as `Queue<T>` 1 + n for its copy. The limit stops the read before the copy or the sort. A structure that implements only `IList<T>`, `ISet<T>`, `IDictionary<K,V>` or `IReadOnlyCollection<T>` is a member too: it is read as the collection of its family over its box, it has no `null`, and `has()` is `true`. A type of the assembly `Great.Parts.Cel` or `Great.Parts.Cel.Protobuf` is not read as an object, a type of another assembly is, whatever its namespace. See [Cost](docs/cost.md#data-of-the-application) and [Types of a type context](docs/type-mapping.md#types-of-a-type-context).
- A set, a collection without an index or a structure that is a collection (other than `ImmutableArray<T>`) is not read as an element, a key or a value of a collection, at any depth: the member that holds it is left out with `GPCEL002`, since the copy would be made at each read of an element, where the limit of cost has no charge. A list, an array or a dictionary is read instead. See [Source generator diagnostics](docs/generator-diagnostics.md#gpcel002).
- The type `decimal`, only on .NET 11: an IEEE 754 decimal128 number of 34 significant digits, with `CelType.Decimal`, `CelValue.FromDecimal` and `TryGetDecimal`, `CelObjectType.FromDecimal`, and the rows of `Decimal128` and `System.Decimal` of the type mapping. An input is exact, with its digits, its exponent and the sign of its zero. A `decimal` result is a `Decimal128` exactly and a `System.Decimal` rounded half to even to 28 places, or `ResultConversion` outside its range. A NaN and an infinity of `Decimal128` are refused by `CelBindings.Add` and are the evaluation error `ConversionRange` where they are read lazily. Two decimals are equal when they are the same number (`1.50 == 1.5`, `-0 == 0`), a decimal equals nothing of another type, and `CelList.CreateSorted` orders decimals by the number. See [Type mapping](docs/type-mapping.md#decimal).
- The extension set `decimal` version 1, only on .NET 11, added with `CelExtensions.Decimal(1)`: the type identifier `decimal` and 18 overloads — `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, the negation and the four orderings of two decimals, `decimal()` of an `int`, a `uint`, a `double`, a `string` and a `decimal`, and `int()`, `uint()`, `double()` and `string()` of a decimal. The arithmetic follows IEEE 754-2019 (the exponent of the result, rounding to 34 digits half to even), an infinite result is `Overflow` and a divisor that is zero is `DivisionByZero` before the division. `decimal('…')` reads the grammar of the page, rounds more than 34 digits half to even and refuses an exponent out of -6176 to 6111 with `ConversionRange`; `decimal(x)` of a decimal is the identity, with its digits and the sign of its zero. A call costs 1, `decimal(double)` 4, and a string that is read or written adds ⌈L/10⌉. The set has no name in an environment description in JSON. See [Decimal](docs/decimal.md), [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-decimal-version-1) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-decimal).
- The literal of `decimal`, only on .NET 11, in two forms: `12.34m`, `.5m`, `1e3m` and `2.5E+3M`, and the integer form `100m`, `0m` and `100M`. The environment reads it after `CelEnvironmentBuilder.EnableDecimalLiterals()`, which needs the extension set `decimal` (`MissingExtensionDependency` without it); without the option, and always on .NET 10, a literal is the parse error `SyntaxNotEnabled`. The value is exact, with its digits, its exponent and the sign of its zero — `string(1e2m)` is `"1E+2"` and `-0m` is a negative zero — and `100m == 1e2m`. A literal of more than 34 significant digits or with an exponent out of -6176 to 6111 is `InvalidLiteral`, and `0x10m` is a syntax error. Without the set, a selection of a field of the type `decimal` is `FieldNotAccessible`, a variable or a function parameter of `System.Decimal` or `Decimal128` is `TypeNotDeclared`, and a value of `decimal` in a place of the type `dyn` is `TypeNotAccessible`. See [Decimal](docs/decimal.md#literals).
- The extension set `optional` version 2: the type `optional_type(T)` and its value `CelOptional<T>`, the functions `optional.of`, `optional.ofNonZeroValue`, `optional.none`, `value`, `hasValue`, `or`, `orValue`, `first`, `last`, `optional.unwrap` and `unwrapOpt`, and the macros `optMap` and `optFlatMap`. `optional.none()` and `optional.of(null)` are different values, and two optionals are equal when both are empty or both hold equal values. `or` and `orValue` evaluate their argument only when the receiver is empty, and `value()` of an empty optional is `OptionalHasNoValue`. A `CelOptional<X>` is a variable, a binding, a parameter and a result by the rows of the type mapping, and `optional_type<E>` is a type record of an environment description in JSON. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-optional-version-2), [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-optional) and [Type mapping](docs/type-mapping.md#optional-values).
- The optional syntax of the extension set `optional`: `e.?f`, `e[?k]`, `[?e]`, `{?k: v}` and `T{?f: v}`. A selection or an index gives an optional — the empty one for a key, a field or an index that is not there — and a list, a map or a message holds a position only when its optional has a value. An optional is selected and indexed through its value (`optional.of({'a': 1}).a` is `optional.of(1)`), `has(a.?b.c)` tests the presence of `c` in the value, and the type checker unwraps an optional operand once. Optional types are joined by their value types, so `[optional.none(), optional.of(1)]` is `list(optional_type(int))`. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#optional-syntax).
- The package `Great.Parts.Cel.Protobuf` describes messages of protobuf to an environment: `CelProtobufTypesBuilder.AddFile` takes a file that `protoc` generated for C# with the files it imports, `AddProtoExtension` takes the typed objects of proto2 extensions, and `Build` gives a `CelProtobufTypes` provider to add with `AddTypes`, with every message and enumeration under its full protobuf name and the well-known types of `google/protobuf` always included. A field is read by its number through the field accessors of `Google.Protobuf`, without reflection: integers, floating point numbers, `bool`, `string`, `bytes` as the memory of the message, an enumeration as `int`, a `repeated` field as a list and a `map` field as a map in the order of insertion, both as views that copy nothing, `oneof` members, a message field that is not set as an empty message, a wrapper as its value or `null`, `Timestamp` and `Duration` as `timestamp` and `duration` (outside the range of CEL the field is `ConversionRange`), and `Struct`, `Value` and `ListValue` as a JSON-like map, value and list. `has()` follows the field selection rules of the language definition for proto2 and proto3, and a proto2 extension is a field with its full name. See [Type mapping](docs/type-mapping.md).
- The extension set `protos` version 0: the macros `proto.getExt(m, pkg.name)` and `proto.hasExt(m, pkg.name)`, which select the extension field with the full name `pkg.name` of a message of proto2 and test its presence; a second argument that is not a chain of identifiers with a dot is `InvalidMacroCall` at the argument. The macros need a type source that describes the extension as a field; without the set the call is an ordinary call. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-protos-version-0) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-protos).
- The type `Any` (`CelType.Any`, `any` in an environment description in JSON) and the literals of messages of protobuf: a type that takes every type, which the type checker treats as `dyn`, and an expression `M{f: v, …}` of a type that a type source makes constructible, checked field by field — a name the type lacks is `NoSuchField` (`FieldNotAccessible` with a reason), a value that does not fit its field `TypeMismatch`, both at the entry — and evaluated in the order of the text, a field named twice keeping its last value, for `1 + n` units and `⌈L/10⌉` more for the strings and bytes it writes. A declaration of the type `Any` needs a description of `google.protobuf.Any`, otherwise it is `TypeNotDeclared`. The names of the thirteen well-known messages that stand for a CEL type — `google.protobuf.Any`, `Value`, `Struct`, `ListValue` and the nine wrappers — are `CelType.ObjectNamed` of that type. `CelObjectType.CreateInstance` throws `NotSupportedException` for a type that does not create objects, and two virtual members, `FieldReadCost` and `EqualityCost`, let a description charge the units of reading a field that grow with the instance and compare its instances itself, charged once. See [Cost](docs/cost.md#nodes-of-an-expression).
- Messages of protobuf are created, unpacked and compared by the package `Great.Parts.Cel.Protobuf`. An object literal `M{f: v, …}` of any message of the provider creates the message with each field set by its kind — a 32-bit integer, a `uint32` and an enumeration outside their range are `ConversionRange`, a float takes the nearest float, a wrapper takes a number and `null` leaves it unset, `Value`, `Struct` and `ListValue` take JSON-like values, a message is shared and not copied — and the literal of a wrapper, `Value`, `Struct`, `ListValue` or `Any` is the value it converts to. A `google.protobuf.Any` is unpacked when a field of that type is read, by the part of its type URL after the last `/` among the messages of the provider and the well-known types, up to 16 `Any` in a chain; one that cannot be unpacked is `InvalidAny` (3020) with the type URL and the reason, and the unpack is charged before the content is parsed. A value written into an `Any` field is packed under `type.googleapis.com/<full name>`. Two messages are compared by CEL equality of their set fields, not by the `Equals` of the generated class: a NaN is not equal to itself, an `Any` is compared by what it holds, and unknown fields are not compared, which differs from cel-go; the comparison costs `1 + ⌈(Sₗ + Sᵣ)/10⌉` for the sizes of the messages. The literals `google.protobuf.Timestamp{…}` and `google.protobuf.Duration{…}` are not supported. See [Protobuf messages](docs/protobuf.md).
- The extension sets `strings` version 5 and `sets` version 0: `charAt`, `indexOf` and `lastIndexOf` with and without an offset, `lowerAscii`, `upperAscii`, `replace`, `split`, `substring`, `trim`, `join`, `reverse` and `strings.quote`, with indexes in code points, and `sets.contains`, `sets.equivalent` and `sets.intersects`. An index or an offset outside the string is `IndexOutOfRange`. The searches take time linear in the lengths of the strings, and `replace`, `split` and `join` are charged as their result is built. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-strings-version-5) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-strings).
- The function `format` of the set `strings` version 5: `s.format(args)` fills the clauses `%s`, `%d`, `%f`, `%e`, `%b`, `%o`, `%x` and `%X`, and `%%` for a single `%`, with the elements of a list, and a precision of at most 100. A call whose format string is a string literal and whose argument is a list literal is checked when the expression is compiled: a wrong format string, an element of a type that does not fit its clause, or more elements than clauses is the error `InvalidFormatString`; any other format error is `InvalidArgument` at evaluation. The result is charged as it is built. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#format) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-strings).
- The extension sets `lists` version 4 and `encoders` version 1: `slice`, `flatten` (one level or down to a depth), `sort`, `reverse`, `distinct`, `lists.range` and the macro `sortBy`, and `base64.decode`, `base64.encode` and `json.encode`. `sort` and `sortBy` order numbers, strings, bytes, `bool` and time by the order of CEL and are stable; a NaN leaves a list in a fixed order that is not a sorted one. `lists.range` takes sizes up to 1,000,000, and `base64.decode` reads a text with or without padding. `json.encode` writes the JSON text as Go does: the keys of a map in order, an integer beyond 2⁵³ - 1 as a string. A result is charged as it is built. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-lists-version-4), [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-lists) and [Limits](docs/limits.md#extension-sets).
- The extension sets `math` version 3 and `bindings` version 1: the macros `math.greatest` and `math.least` (any number of numeric arguments; arguments that are not numbers are `InvalidMacroCall` at the argument), `math.ceil`, `floor`, `round`, `trunc`, `isInf`, `isNaN`, `isFinite`, `abs` (the least `int` is `Overflow`), `sign`, `sqrt`, `bitAnd`, `bitOr`, `bitXor`, `bitNot`, `bitShiftLeft` and `bitShiftRight` (a negative offset is `InvalidArgument`), and the macro `cel.bind`, which names a value once for its result. The function `cel.@block` is in `bindings` for trees that the library builds itself, since it has no public way to build a syntax tree: a slot is evaluated at its first read and not more than once, and a slot that reads itself reads the slot of the same number of the next enclosing block that has it, as in cel-go, and is `Unbound` when no block is left. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-math-version-3) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-math).
- The extension set `two-var-comprehensions` version 0: the macros `all`, `exists`, `existsOne`, `exists_one`, `transformList`, `transformMap` and `transformMapEntry` with the names of two iteration variables — the index and the element of a list, the key and the value of a map — and, for trees that the library builds itself, the function `cel.@mapInsert`. The forms of three and four arguments are the macros of the set; `all`, `exists`, `exists_one`, `map` and `filter` of two and three arguments stay the standard macros. A name that is not a simple identifier, two equal names and the name of the accumulator are `InvalidMacroCall`; a key that `transformMapEntry` puts in twice is `DuplicateMapKey`. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-two-var-comprehensions-version-0) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-two-var-comprehensions).
- The extension set `network` version 1: the abstract types `net.IP` and `net.CIDR` and the functions `ip`, `cidr`, `isIP`, `isCIDR`, `ip.isCanonical`, `string`, `family`, `isLoopback`, `isUnspecified`, `isLinkLocalUnicast`, `isLinkLocalMulticast`, `isGlobalUnicast`, `containsIP`, `containsCIDR`, `isMask`, `masked`, `prefixLength` and `.ip()` of a prefix. Parsing is strict, as in `net/netip` of Go: an IPv4-mapped IPv6 address and a zone are `InvalidArgument`, while `isCIDR` accepts the host bits of a prefix; a constant argument of `ip` and `cidr` is checked at evaluation, not at compilation. The names of the types are type identifiers and can declare a variable or a function of the application (`CelType.ObjectNamed("net.IP")`); `CelTypeKind.Abstract` and `CelValueKind.Abstract` are the new kinds of such a type and of its values, which an application receives as a `CelValue`. See [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md#set-network-version-1) and [Cost](docs/cost.md#set-network).
- Extension sets in an environment: `CelEnvironmentBuilder.AddExtension` collects them, `CelExtension.Create` builds a set of the application from function declarations with their implementations and the names of the sets it needs, and the members of `CelExtensions` hand out the set of a name and an explicit version. `Build` checks the sets before the other declarations: a version that the set does not have is `UnknownExtensionVersion` with the versions it has, one name in two versions is `ExtensionVersionConflict`, two different sets with one name and version are `DuplicateExtension`, and a set that needs another that is not added is `MissingExtensionDependency`; a set that fails one of these checks is left out of the others. Overloads of the sets are checked with the standard library and the application — `OverlappingOverloads`, `StandardLibraryConflict`, `DuplicateOverloadId` — and a function of the application with the form of a macro of a set is `StandardLibraryConflict`. The same set added twice is one set, the order of the calls changes neither the environment nor the errors, and the macros of a set expand only in an environment that has it. The key `extensions` of an environment description in JSON adds a set of the catalog by name and version, and the build error of its element has the place of the element in the JSON text. The rules are on the page [Extension sets](docs/extensions.md).
- The standard library functions `matches` and `matches_string`, `matches(s, re)` and `s.matches(re)`: whether some part of the string matches the regular expression, with the syntax and semantics of RE2, found in time linear in the length of the string. A pattern that is a string literal is parsed and built once, when the expression is compiled, and a pattern that is refused — a syntax error, a construct that RE2 does not have, a count, a height or a size over its limit — is the compilation error `InvalidRegex` at the literal. Any other pattern, such as a variable or `dyn('…')`, is prepared when the call is evaluated, through a cache of patterns that each environment has, shared by its programs and empty at first, also for the environment that `ToBuilder` gives; its refusal is the evaluation error `EvalInvalidRegex` at the call. The cache never changes a result or a cost. A call costs the match, `1 + ⌈(n + 1) · m/10⌉` for a subject of `n` UTF-16 code units and a program of size `m`, and for a pattern that is not a literal also its parse, `1 + ⌈L/10⌉` for a pattern of `L` code units, and its build, `⌈m/4⌉`; each is charged before the work it pays for.
- The standard library functions of time: `timestamp` and `duration` from a value of their own type, from a string (RFC 3339 and the duration grammar of Go) and, for `timestamp`, from the Unix seconds; `int` and `string` of a timestamp or a duration; `+` and `-` of timestamps and durations; `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=` of two timestamps or two durations; and the identifiers of types `google.protobuf.Timestamp` and `google.protobuf.Duration`. A sum or a difference outside the range is `Overflow`, a number or a string outside the range of its type `ConversionRange`, and a string that does not read `InvalidArgument`. A call such as `timestamp('2009-02-13T23:31:30Z')` on a string literal is read once at compilation; its value, its cost and its error are those of the call.
- The receivers of `timestamp` and `duration` — `getFullYear`, `getMonth`, `getDayOfYear`, `getDayOfMonth`, `getDate`, `getDayOfWeek`, `getHours`, `getMinutes`, `getSeconds` and `getMilliseconds` — with a time zone name or without one. A name is `UTC`, a fixed offset `[+-]HH:MM` or an identifier of the time zone database of the environment; a name that is none of them is the evaluation error `TimeZoneNotFound`, and a name with `:` that is no offset `InvalidTimeZone`. Without a zone the local time is UTC; the milliseconds of a duration are those within its second.
- `CelEnvironmentBuilder.UseTimeZones`: the time zone database of an environment, kept by `CelEnvironment.ToBuilder`. A different database added to the same environment is the build error `TimeZoneDatabaseConflict`.
- The pages [Standard library](docs/standard-library.md#time), [Type mapping](docs/type-mapping.md#time), [Cost](docs/cost.md#time), [Limits](docs/limits.md#values) and [Error codes](docs/error-codes.md#messages-of-time) describe time, and the recipe [Time zone database](docs/recipes/time-zone-database.md) shows how to give an environment the zones that `getHours('Europe/Moscow')` needs.
- `CelTimeZoneDatabase`: `Create` reads and checks the TZif files (version 2 or later, RFC 9636) of the application, with their POSIX TZ footer rules, and refuses data that is not valid, has leap second records, or repeats an identifier with other data; `Empty`, `Version`, `ZoneIds` (in ordinal order) and `Contains` are implemented. The database is immutable and shared between threads.
- Values of time: `CelTimestamp` (a moment in UTC from 0001-01-01 to 9999-12-31 with nanoseconds) and `CelDuration` (a `long` of nanoseconds), with their equality, order and exact text, and the values `timestamp` and `duration` in variables, fields of objects, list elements, map values and results. `DateTimeOffset`, `DateTime` of the kind `Utc`, `TimeSpan`, `CelTimestamp`, `CelDuration` and the `Nullable<>` of each convert exactly in both directions, never by rounding: a `DateTime` of another kind is refused by `CelBindings.Add` with `ArgumentException`, a `TimeSpan` outside the range of `duration` is the evaluation error `ConversionRange`, and a result whose nanoseconds are not a multiple of 100 cannot be a `DateTimeOffset` or a `TimeSpan`. A typed collection of time, such as `IReadOnlyList<DateTimeOffset>`, is not a row of the table: time in a collection goes through `CelList`, `CelMap`, `CelValue` or `object`.
- The whole environment description in JSON, read by `CelEnvironmentJson.Apply`: the keys `container` (`UseContainer`), `variables` with a type record in full (`list<int>`, `map<string, acme.Order>`, wrappers, `null_type`, `type<E>`, `any`; `type_name` with `params`), `functions` (`AddFunction` with overloads, global or on a `target`, with type parameters such as `~A`, without implementations, which `AddFunctionImplementation` registers by the overload `id`), `extensions` (checked; the catalog is empty until a set is added) and `greatPartsCel.objectTypes` (one provider of types over dictionaries, `CelObjectTypeBuilder.ForDictionary`); `timestamp` and `duration` are type records, and `optional_type<E>` is the optional type, which needs the extension set `optional`. A description that has errors changes nothing and returns every error `InvalidConfiguration` in the order of the text. A build error of a declaration of a description names it as `environment JSON '<name>'` and carries its place in the JSON text in `CelError.SourceLocation`: for example `DuplicateVariable`, `DuplicateType`, `ContainerConflict`, `InvalidVariableName`, `DuplicateOverloadId`, `OverlappingOverloads`, `StandardLibraryConflict`, and `MissingImplementation`, `ImplementationSignatureMismatch` and `TypeNotDeclared`, whose text names no source. When two declarations conflict, the one that sorts first by its source is named first, whatever the order of the calls.
- Collections of the application and `JsonElement` in bindings, variables and results: `CelList.CreateView`, `CelList.CreateCopy`, `CelList.CreateSorted` and `CelMap.CreateView` (lazy views with an element converter, a copy, a set sorted in the order of CEL, and a dictionary whose key is found back by one lookup; a `Guid` key is found only by its canonical text); `CelBindings.Add<T>` and `CelEnvironmentBuilder.AddVariable<T>` take arrays, lists, sets, other collections and dictionaries of the listed element and key types, classes of the application as bare objects and `JsonElement` as `dyn`, and refuse another type with `TypeNotDeclared` or `ArgumentException`. A JSON array and object are read lazily, a number is a `double`, and a repeated property name of an object is the error `DuplicateMapKey` wherever it is touched. A .NET collection that no type source describes is `TypeNotAccessible` at the node that reads it; one that a source describes is an object. An exception of a view of the application is `ApplicationException` where its element is read.
- Object types of the application: `CelObjectType`, `CelObjectField`, `CelEnumType` and `CelTypeProvider`, added to an environment with `CelEnvironmentBuilder.AddTypes`; types described at run time with `CelObjectTypeBuilder<TCarrier>` over a carrier class and `CelObjectTypeBuilder.ForDictionary` over a dictionary, whose field without a value is `FieldNotSet` and whose value of another type than declared is `FieldTypeMismatch`; `CelValue.FromObject` and `CelValue.TryGetObject`. Two different descriptions of one type name or of one .NET type, or a type named as a type identifier of the standard library, are the build error `DuplicateType`, and an object type in the type of a field that no source describes is `TypeNotDeclared`. A .NET collection in a place of the .NET type `object` is the evaluation error `TypeNotAccessible`.
- Types that two type contexts share: descriptions of one .NET type with the same name and the same fields (names and types, in order), or enumerations with the same name and constants, from two or more `CelTypeContext` sources of one environment are one declaration, not `DuplicateType`, whichever order `AddTypes` is called in; the description of the source whose name is smaller, ordinal, stays. Descriptions that differ, a source that is not a `CelTypeContext`, and two descriptions of one source are still `DuplicateType`. A value paired with the description that was dropped is read, compared and named by `type()` as the one that stays, and fits `T` and `list(T)`.
- Field selection and `has()` on objects of the application, by the field number that type checking found, with the type checking error `NoSuchField` for a field the type does not have and the evaluation errors `FieldNotSet`, `FieldTypeMismatch`, `EvalNoSuchField` for a value of type `dyn`, and `TypeNotAccessible` for an instance that no type source describes; reading a field is an event of cancellation, and an exception of a read is `ApplicationException`. A variable or a field of an object type takes an instance of its class or carrier class. The equality of objects compares the fields of one description, and objects of different descriptions are unequal; `type()` of an object is its object type, and enumeration constants are `int` identifiers such as `acme.Status.Active`. An object value is the result for a requested class, struct or interface of the application that its instance is, and for `object`. A message literal is the type checking error `NotAType` for a name that is no object type and `TypeNotConstructible` for an object type.
- Evaluation of field selection and `has()` on maps, `&&`, `?:` and the standard macros `all`, `exists`, `exists_one`, `map` and `filter`, with the errors `EvalNoSuchField`, `FieldSelectionNotSupported` and `EvalInvalidComprehensionRange`.
- `CelEvalOptions.CostLimit`: the cost limit of one evaluation; an operation whose cost would exceed it does not run, and the evaluation ends with `CostLimitExceeded`. Without a limit no cost is counted.
- Cancellation of an evaluation: a token that can be canceled is checked before the first call of an application function or macro step and then every 64; `CancellationToken.None` counts nothing.
- Checks of the values that enter and leave an evaluation, at any depth: a binding of another type than its variable is `BindingTypeMismatch`, an argument of an application function that does not fit its parameter is `EvalNoMatchingOverload`, and a result of another type than declared is `ImplementationResultMismatch`. An exception of application code is `ApplicationException`, an exception of the library `InternalError`, and a stack without room `InsufficientStack`.
- An error value that an element, a key or a value of a collection of the application is, is not a mismatch of type: when a read of a variable, a call that checks an argument or a result, or the conversion of a result meets it, the error is the value read, with the place of the reading node — the first one in the order of the walk, a list by index and a map in the order of iteration. The same holds for an iteration variable of a macro: an error element gets the place of the node that reads it.
- A class, record, struct or interface of the application in `AddVariable<T>` and in the delegate forms of `AddFunction` is the object type that a type source describes for exactly that .NET type, resolved by `CelEnvironmentBuilder.Build`; without a description it is `TypeNotDeclared`, as before.
- A class, record, struct or interface of the application as a parameter or the result of `AddFunctionImplementation<T1, …, TResult>` is the object type that a type source describes for exactly that .NET type, compared with the declared overload by `CelEnvironmentBuilder.Build`; without a description it is `TypeNotDeclared`, not `ImplementationSignatureMismatch`.
- A list of a class of the application — `IReadOnlyList<Class>`, `Class[]` or `IList<Class>` — as the result of `AddFunctionImplementation` or of a delegate form of `AddFunction` is `list(Class)`, with the object type that a type source describes for the class, and also fits a declared `list(dyn)`; without a description of the class it is `TypeNotDeclared`. As a parameter it is `ImplementationSignatureMismatch`, since a list of a class has no expected type.
- The cost of application data: a lookup in a JSON object (selection, index, `has()`) charges its `n` properties, `size`, `in`, a comprehension and the equality of JSON objects charge each property by the length of its name before it is processed, the index of a JSON array charges its length, a string read from a JSON document charges `1 + ⌈L/10⌉` for the length `L` of its token before the string is created, and the enumeration of a map view ordered by ascending key charges its sort before the first entry. The copy of a set or of a collection without an index that an application function returns is not checked against the declared result type at depth, as the type of its input row already fits it.
- Evaluation without type checking takes a dictionary bound to a variable of an object type as the value of that type, as the evaluation with type checking does.
- Compilation and evaluation of an expression: `CelEnvironmentBuilder`, `CelEnvironment.Compile<T>` and `CelProgram<T>.Evaluate`, with the result converted to the requested .NET type.
- Literals of the types `int`, `uint`, `double`, `string`, `bytes`, `bool` and `null`, list literals and map literals; a map is enumerated in the order of its literal.
- The standard library functions of logic, equality, arithmetic, ordering and conversion: `!`, `&&`, `||`, `?:`, `==` and `!=`; `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%` and negation of `int`, `uint` and `double`, and `+` of strings, bytes and lists; `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=` of `bool`, numbers, strings and bytes, also across `int`, `uint` and `double` when an operand is `dyn`; `type`, `dyn`, `bool`, `bytes`, `double`, `int`, `string` and `uint`; and the type identifiers such as `int` and `list`. Integer overflow is the error `Overflow`, a zero divisor `DivisionByZero` or `ModulusByZero`, a conversion outside the range of its type `ConversionRange`, and a text that does not parse `InvalidArgument`; numbers are parsed and written without the culture of the process.
- The standard library functions of lists, maps and strings: indexing of lists and maps, `in`, `size` of strings, bytes, lists and maps, and `contains`, `startsWith` and `endsWith`. A list index outside the list is the error `IndexOutOfRange`, an index of the type `double` that is not an integer `InvalidArgument`, and a missing key `NoSuchKey`; a map finds a key of another numeric type with an equal value, so `{1: 'a'}[dyn(1.0)]` is `'a'`. An index of an operand of the type `dyn` is chosen by the kind of its value, so `dyn([7])[0u]` is `7`. The size of a string counts code points, and `contains` finds a substring in time linear in the lengths of the string and the substring.
- Variables declared in code or in an environment description in JSON (`CelEnvironmentJson`).
- Parse, type and evaluation errors as results, not exceptions, each with its code of `CelErrorCode`, its stage and its place in the expression.
- Parsing of the complete CEL syntax: the conditional operator `?:`, `&&`, comparisons and `in`, subtraction, multiplication and remainder, logical not, field selection, also of escaped field names in backticks, indexing, receiver-style calls, message literals and names with a leading dot. A message literal is parsed.
- The parse errors `ReservedIdentifier`, for a reserved word such as `if` used as a name, and `SyntaxNotEnabled`, for the optional syntax `.?`, `[?` and `?` without its extension set and for `decimal` literals such as `1.5m`.
- Parsing of the standard macros `has`, `all`, `exists`, `exists_one`, `map` with two or three arguments and `filter`, with the parse error `InvalidMacroCall` for a call that a macro cannot expand, such as `xs.all(1, true)` or `has(x)`.
- The limits of parsing for untrusted text, on the new page [Limits](docs/limits.md): 65,536 UTF-8 bytes of text (`ExpressionTooLong`), a nesting and a syntax tree depth of 250 (`NestingTooDeep`), 100 errors (`TooManyErrors`), and `InsufficientStack` instead of a stack overflow on a thread with a small stack.
- Parsing reports every error of a text, up to the limit, in the order of the text: after a syntax error it goes on at the next comma or closing bracket. An unexpected exception inside the parser becomes the error `InternalError` with the original exception.
- `CelCompileOptions.SourceLocation`: the position at which an expression starts in its source document. Every compilation and evaluation error of the expression then has its position in the document in `CelError.SourceLocation`.
- The type model of CEL: `CelType.WrapperOf`, `CelType.TypeOf`, `CelType.ObjectNamed` and `CelType.TypeParameter`; wrappers are written `wrapper(int)` in messages.
- Type values: `CelValue.FromType` and `CelValue.TryGetType`; a type value compares by the kind and the name of its type, so the type value of `list(int)` equals that of `list`.
- `CelValue.CelEquals`, the CEL equality: `int`, `uint` and `double` on one number line, a NaN equal to nothing, strings by code units, lists and maps by their elements.
- Variables and bindings of enumerations, `[Flags]` included, as `int`; of nullable value types as wrappers such as `wrapper(int)`; and of `CelValue`, `CelList`, `CelMap` and `object`. An enumeration on `ulong` above the range of `int` is the evaluation error `ConversionRange`.
- Functions of the application declared with `CelEnvironmentBuilder.AddFunction` — from a .NET delegate, or as a `CelFunctionDecl` of overloads called globally or on a receiver, with type parameters and a cost function (`CelOverloadDecl.WithCost`) — and implemented with `AddFunctionImplementation`. A typed delegate gets its arguments converted to its .NET types; an argument that does not convert is the evaluation error `ArgumentConversion`.
- Build errors of an environment, all reported by `CelEnvironmentBuilder.Build` in an order that does not depend on the order of the calls: `DuplicateVariable`, `OverlappingOverloads`, `StandardLibraryConflict`, `MissingImplementation`, `TypeNotDeclared`, `UnusedImplementation`, `ImplementationSignatureMismatch`, `DuplicateOverloadId`, `DuplicateImplementation`, `InvalidFunctionName`, `InvalidVariableName` and `ContainerConflict`. The same declaration repeated is one declaration.
- `CelEnvironmentBuilder.UseContainer`, the name resolution container of an environment; `CelEnvironment.ToBuilder`, a builder with the declarations of an environment; `CelStandardLibrary.Version`.
- Type checking of the complete syntax by the gradual type checking of the CEL language definition: type parameters of overloads deduced at each call, also through type values, receiver-style calls, field selection on maps and `dyn`, indexes, the standard macros and their variables, the type identifiers such as `int` and `list`, and names resolved in the container, the longest qualified name first. A compilation returns every check error, ordered by place, with the new errors `TypeMismatch`, for an operand of `&&` or `||` that is not a `bool`, `NoSuchField`, for a selection on a type without fields, and `InvalidComprehensionRange`, for a macro over a value that is not a list or a map; `InsufficientStack` and `InternalError` end type checking instead of an exception.
- `CelCompileOptions.ExpectedType`, and the expected type that `T` of `Compile<T>` implies: an expression whose type is not assignable to it is the compilation error `ExpectedTypeMismatch`, and an expected type that `T` cannot hold throws `ArgumentException` before the text is read.
- `CelEnvironment.Evaluate<T>`: compilation and evaluation in one call, with the result and the errors of `Compile<T>` followed by `CelProgram<T>.Evaluate`; the token is checked before the compilation and between the two steps.
- `CelCompilationCache`: a bounded, thread-safe cache of compilations of one environment, with a capacity of entries and of the total text length; unsuccessful compilations are kept too, and `CelCompilationCache.Evaluate<T>` is the one-step call through the cache.
- Results of the types `IReadOnlyList<X>`, `IReadOnlyDictionary<XK, XV>`, `CelList`, `CelMap`, `CelType`, `Nullable<X>` and `object`: a list or a map is a lazy view when no element can fail its conversion, and an immutable copy otherwise. Before a lazy view the value is checked against the type that `T` reads, with `null` allowed where the view reads a null reference, such as an element of `IReadOnlyList<string>`, so a view and a copy give the same result. A `T` outside the type mapping, such as `short` or `Guid`, throws `ArgumentException`.
- Parameters of typed delegates of the types `IReadOnlyList<X>` and `IReadOnlyDictionary<XK, XV>` in `AddFunctionImplementation`. The CEL type of a parameter is the one the expected type of `Compile<T>` gives its .NET type and must be the declared one: `Guid`, `short`, `float` or an enumeration, also in the delegate form of `AddFunction`, is `ImplementationSignatureMismatch` when the environment is built. A declared `wrapper(string)` or `wrapper(bytes)` also takes a `string` or `byte[]` parameter or result, with `null` as a null reference. With a cost limit, a parameter that is a copy is charged before the call: a list of n elements 1 + n, a map of n pairs 1 + 2n and ⌈L/10⌉ for each string key of length L, and a `byte[]` of L bytes 1 + ⌈L/10⌉; a lazy view and `ReadOnlyMemory<byte>` cost nothing.
